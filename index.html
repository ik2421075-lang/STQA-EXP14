Medical Information

Understanding COVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2)

A comprehensive medical overview of etiology, pathogenesis, and transmission biology.

What is Coronavirus?

Coronavirus disease (COVID-19) is an infectious respiratory illness caused by the novel coronavirus SARS-CoV-2 (Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus 2). First identified in late 2019, it attacks respiratory epithelium and can cause systemic symptoms.

  • Belongs to Coronaviridae family of single-stranded RNA viruses
  • Enters host cells via ACE-2 receptors in alveolar tissues
  • Can cause mild upper respiratory symptoms or acute viral pneumonia

How Does It Spread?

The virus spreads mainly between people who are in close physical contact with each other, primarily through respiratory droplets expelled when an infected person breathes, speaks, coughs, sneezes, or sings.

  • Respiratory Droplets: Heavy droplets landing directly on mucosal surfaces
  • Aerosol Particles: Microscopic droplets lingering in poorly ventilated spaces
  • Fomite Transmission: Touching contaminated surfaces followed by mouth, eyes, or nose

Incubation & Clinical Course

The incubation period typically ranges from 2 to 14 days following exposure, with most infected individuals developing symptoms between day 4 and day 7. Asymptomatic carriers can also transmit the virus.

  • Early Phase (Days 1–5): Upper respiratory symptoms, fever, malaise
  • Pulmonary Phase (Days 5–10): Persistent cough, potential dyspnea
  • Convalescence (Days 10–14): Resolution of viral replication and recovery
Clinical Manifestations

COVID-19 Symptoms & Self-Check

Identify mild, moderate, and critical clinical indicators according to WHO classifications.

Most Common

Initial Symptoms

  • High Fever or Chills

    Body temperature exceeding 38°C (100.4°F)

  • Persistent Dry Cough

    Continuous coughing fit lasting over an hour

  • Fatigue & Weakness

    Unusual physical exhaustion and lethargy

  • Anosmia / Ageusia

    Sudden loss of sense of smell or taste

Less Common

Secondary Signs

  • Sore Throat

    Irritation and pain when swallowing

  • Severe Headache

    Frontal or generalized persistent headache

  • Generalized Muscle Aches

    Myalgia affecting back and limbs

  • Gastrointestinal Distress

    Diarrhea, nausea, or abdominal cramping

EMERGENCY

Critical Red Flags

  • Dyspnea (Shortness of Breath)

    Difficulty breathing even while resting

  • Persistent Chest Pressure

    Continuous pain or tightness across chest

  • Hypoxia (SpO2 < 94%)

    Drop in blood oxygen saturation levels

  • Confusion & Cyanosis

    Bluish lips, pale nail beds, mental disorientation

Seek Emergency Care: If you or anyone experiences critical symptoms, dial 108 or 1075 immediately.

Protection & Safety

Evidence-Based Prevention Protocols

Practical, scientifically proven steps to protect yourself, your family, and your workplace.

Wear Certified Masks

Wear well-fitted multi-layer masks or N95 respirators in enclosed indoor environments, crowded public transit, and medical settings.

Frequent Hand Hygiene

Clean your hands thoroughly with soap and water for at least 20 seconds, or sanitize using alcohol-based rub (>70% alcohol).

Physical Distancing

Maintain at least 6 feet (2 meters) distance from individuals who are coughing, sneezing, or displaying flu-like symptoms.

Improve Ventilation

Open windows and doors to enhance fresh airflow indoors. Prefer outdoor or well-ventilated gatherings over sealed rooms.

Disinfect High-Touch Surfaces

Routinely sanitize doorknobs, switches, mobile phone surfaces, laptops, and shared study or workstation desks.

Complete Vaccination

Get vaccinated with primary doses and precautionary booster shots according to local health authority guidelines.

Immunization Drive

COVID-19 Vaccination Guide

Approved vaccine formulations, dosing schedules, and debunking common misconceptions.

Recombinant Vector

Covishield (AstraZeneca)

Recombinant, replication-deficient chimpanzee adenovirus vector encoding the SARS-CoV-2 Spike glycoprotein.

Doses: 2 doses Gap: 84 days Efficacy: ~81% against hospitalization
Inactivated Whole Virion

Covaxin (BBV152)

Whole-virion inactivated Vero cell technology providing broad multi-antigen immunity against variant spike mutations.

Doses: 2 doses Gap: 28 days Efficacy: ~78% against symptomatic COVID
Protein Subunit

Corbevax (Recombinant)

Recombinant protein subunit vaccine containing the Receptor Binding Domain (RBD) formulated with adjuvant.

Doses: 2 doses + Booster Gap: 28 days Target: Approved for ages 12+ and booster

Vaccine Myths vs. Scientific Facts

MYTH "The COVID-19 vaccine alters human genetic DNA."
FACT

COVID-19 vaccines do not enter the nucleus of human cells where DNA is housed. They deliver instructions to trigger antibodies and degrade naturally within hours.

MYTH "If I already had COVID-19, I do not need the vaccine."
FACT

Natural immunity diminishes over time. Hybrid immunity (combining prior infection with vaccination) provides significantly stronger protection against reinfection and hospitalization.
